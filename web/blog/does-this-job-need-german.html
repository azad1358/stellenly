Does this job really need German? How to read a German job ad

English-speaking" doesn't always mean no German. The exact phrases that tell you whether German is required, nice to have, or truly not needed.

AAzad Published 26 Oct 2026Updated 27 Oct 2026, 19:50 CET · live data2 min read

The most common frustration for internationals in Germany: a job ad written in English, a team that "works in English", and then the interview starts in German. The good news is that the wording of the ad almost always tells you what to expect, if you know which phrases to look for.

Right now, 11% of the 2,037 English-language jobs on this site either don't require German or list it only as a plus.

The four levels, and the phrases behind them

What it says What it usually means
"No German required", "German is not necessary", "our working language is English" Not required. You can do the job in English.
"German is a plus", "nice to have", "basic German", "willingness to learn German" Nice to have. You can get the job without it; it helps with colleagues and promotions.
No mention of German at all Unclear. Often fine for tech roles; ask early in the process.
"Fluent German", "German C1", "business-fluent German and English", "verhandlungssicheres Deutsch" Required. Usually customer-facing, legal, sales or public-sector roles. Don't apply unless you're at that level.

We read every job ad sentence by sentence and show one of these four labels on each job. So "fluent English required, German is a plus" is labelled nice to have, not required.

Watch out for these traps

  • "English and German" in one sentence usually means both are required.
  • Language levels: B2 is "independent user", C1 is "proficient". If an ad asks for B2/C1 German, the team will speak German at least some of the time.
  • German job titles like Sachbearbeiter, Kundenberater or Vertriebsmitarbeiter usually mean German-language work, even if the ad is translated.
  • "m/w/d" in a title has nothing to do with language. It means "male/female/diverse" and is just a legal requirement in German job ads.

Should you learn German anyway?

Yes, even if your job is in English. B1 German makes everyday life (doctors, landlords, the Ausländerbehörde) much easier, and it opens up many more jobs for your next move. Free and paid options:
