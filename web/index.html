          Frequently asked questions

          Can I get a job in Germany without speaking German?
          Yes. Only 28% of the 4,600 English-speaking jobs on Stellenly require German; 14% say it's not needed or just a plus, and most of the rest don't mention it, which usually means the team works in English. Tick No German needed above to see only those jobs.
          How do you know whether a job needs German?
          We read every posting and show its German level on the card: not required, nice to have, not mentioned or required, with the exact sentence from the posting in the job details. We don't hide jobs that need German, so you can decide yourself. How we read job postings.
          Which jobs offer visa sponsorship?
          Right now 274 jobs mention visa sponsorship or work-permit support. Tick Visa sponsorship above. Most non-EU candidates need a work permit, usually the EU Blue Card.
          How much do I need to earn for an EU Blue Card in 2026?
          At least €51k gross a year, or €46k for IT and other shortage jobs, health care and recent graduates. Jobs whose listed salary reaches it are marked Blue Card ✓. Read the Blue Card guide.
          Which German cities have the most English-speaking jobs?
          Berlin (1,616), Munich (1,084), Hamburg (175), Parsdorf (101), Gilching (100). All states and cities.
          Is Stellenly free? Do I need an account?
          It's free, and there is no sign-up: we store no personal data and set no cookies. Saved jobs stay in your browser. You apply directly on the employer's site.
          Where do the jobs come from, and how fresh are they?
          From company career pages and public job APIs, updated every 6 hours. Jobs that disappear from their source are removed automatically, and visitors can report a closed job with 🚩.
          Can I get new jobs sent to me?
          Yes, without an email address: follow our Telegram channel for new jobs every day, or join the community on Discord.
